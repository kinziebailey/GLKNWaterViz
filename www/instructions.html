Introduction

The National Park Service preserves unimpaired the natural and cultural resources and values of the National Park System. The Great Lakes Region Inventory and Monitoring Network (GLKN) is part of the National Park Service Inventory and Monitoring Division, which conducts science in National Parks. GLKN conducts science, like water quality monitoring, in the National Park Service's Great Lakes Region, which extends from the boreal forests of northern Minnesota to the sand dunes of southern Lake Michigan, and includes 72 miles of the Mississippi River and more than 200 miles of the St. Croix and Namekagon Rivers. Despite the network's Great Lakes designation, these parks fall into three watersheds: the Arctic Ocean, the Great Lakes, and the Mississippi River/Gulf of Mexico.

Parameters

As of January 2025, NCRN monitors 15 numeric water quality parameters. NCRN also collects text and categorical data (e.g., epilithic algae color) which are present in the full dataset but absent from this website. Over time, NCRN has added and removed parameters from its monitoring protocol. Table 5 in NCRN's Water Chemistry and Quantity Monitoring Protocol details changes to sampling parameters.

  1. Water temperature
  2. Air pressure (i.e. barometric pressure)
  3. Dissolved Oxygen concentration (DO concentration mg/L)
  4. Dissolved Oxygen saturation (DO saturation %)
  5. Specific Conductance
  6. Conductivity
  7. Salinity
  8. pH
  9. Acid Neutralizing Capacity (ANC)
  10. Total Nitrogen concentration (TN)
  11. Total Phosphorus concentration (TP)
  12. Discharge
  13. Wetted width (stream width across stream crossection where discharge was measured)
  14. Stream Velocity (average water velocity across stream crossection where discharge was measured)
  15. Depth (average water depth across stream crossection where discharge was measured)
